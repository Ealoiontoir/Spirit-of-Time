02 The Centre

A workshop inside a boiler house

The work was done at Rediscover Furniture, the furniture department of the Rediscovery Centre in Ballymun — Ireland's National Centre for the Circular Economy, housed in the former boiler house that once heated the Ballymun flats.

The building makes the argument first: an obsolete industrial structure kept and re-purposed rather than demolished. Inside, the same logic runs at the scale of objects — paint remixed, bicycles refurbished, textiles re-cut, and furniture that would otherwise be skipped repaired and returned to use.

The department works as a social enterprise and as a school. Alongside commissioned restoration it runs workshops and courses, so the skill leaves the building along with the furniture. Its stated aim is to give discarded pieces “a whole new story to tell.”

Working principles

  • Reuse before disposal. The greenest object is the one already made.
  • Repair before replacement. Most failure is local, not total.
  • Skill as infrastructure. Knowledge shared through work outlasts any single piece.

03 The Process

What was done

Nine stages, in the order they happened. Each one is visible somewhere in the compilation and the film above.

  1. 01

    Dismantled

    Cover, fillings and old fixings removed in reverse order of assembly, so nothing was lost that could serve as a pattern later.

  2. 02

    Structure inspected

    With the frame exposed, every joint was tested. The carcass was sound; what had failed were the consumable layers.

  3. 03

    Webbing renewed

    New support webbing strained across the rails and interlaced in both directions — the tensioned plane the whole seat depends on.

  4. 04

    Frame strengthened

    Where movement was found, joints were cleaned and re-secured. Reinforcement was added only where the frame asked for it.

  5. 05

    Timber restored

    The exposed wood was cleaned and sanded by hand, then fed and protected with furniture wax — depth returned, gloss withheld.

  6. 06

    Rebuilt in layers

    Base and padding fitted in sequence — the red and yellow working layers in the photographs — until the chair's original volume came back.

  7. 07

    Upholstered

    Quality fabrics selected and cut: a calm neutral through the centre, the floral on the wings and sides.

  8. 08

    Piping made and fitted

    Piping made by hand from cord and bias-cut cloth, then set into the seams to draw the chair's outline.

  9. 09

    Fitted and hand-stitched

    Panels eased around the curves, larger sections sewn to the body, closing seams slip-stitched by hand. Final assembly.

05 Materials

Four specimens

Traditional richness held in check by contemporary restraint: a calm neutral ground, a decisive floral, a line of contrast piping, and timber allowed to be timber.

Specimen 01

Neutral ground cloth

A quiet, close-woven cloth in a warm neutral, used across the seat, inside back and main body panels. It carries the volume without asking for attention.

Specimen 02

Decorative floral

A high-quality patterned textile with real character and depth of colour, placed on the wings and side panels — strong enough to carry the chair, used sparingly enough not to overwhelm it.

Specimen 03

Hand-made piping

Cord wrapped in a bias-cut strip of cloth, sewn and set into the seams. Structural as well as decorative: it strengthens the join and draws the outline.

Specimen 04

Waxed show wood

The chair's original timber, cleaned, sanded by hand and protected with wax. Not refinished and not stained to a new colour — fed, sealed, burnished.

Swatches are rendered from the page palette — an indication of the scheme, not a colour-accurate record of the cloth.

06 The Detail

The arm supports

This chair is not carried by generic straight armrests. Its exposed side timbers have a specific movement, and it is the single detail that most distinguishes the piece.

  • Rises near the front — giving the arm its height and the chair its stance.
  • Moves slightly backward beneath the arm — so the timber reads as supporting rather than edging.
  • Tapers to a sharper rearward character — narrowing into a keener line towards the wing.

The restoration kept the chair's original proportions rather than turning it into a generic modern piece. This geometry is the reason why.

Machines are good at repetition. A chair that has already lived is not repetitive.

07 Final Setting

A room that takes it seriously

The chair is shown here in a presidential office — a staged composition, and the last room in this record. Not decoration: an argument about standing.

We are used to the idea that dignity belongs to new things, and that a repaired object is a compromise kept out of sight. Placing this chair in a room built for formality inverts that. A restored object is not a lesser object. It is an object with more behind it.

Typology
Wing chair — a tall-backed upholstered easy chair whose wings, historically also called cheeks, project between back and arms to shelter the sitter from draughts. The form emerged in England in the late seventeenth century.
